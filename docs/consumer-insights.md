# Consumer Insights

## 01. The Consumer Tension

The project started from a simple observation: fragrance can create an immediate impression, yet that experience can fade quickly and feel generic across users.

The final deck framed three opportunity areas:

### Longevity
The submission cites a **76% consumer-priority figure for longevity** and contrasts intended long wear with traditional spray formats that may require reapplication.

### Personalization
The research examples point toward a desire for fragrance that feels distinctive to the wearer rather than identical across consumers.

### Sustainability & Experience
The submission also highlights demand for more natural ingredients, ethical sourcing and differentiated beauty experiences.

> **Strategic implication:** The opportunity is not simply to make fragrance louder or stronger. It is to rethink how fragrance is delivered and experienced.

## 02. From Problem to Opportunity

The team's central reframing was:

> **“Fragrance today sits on skin — it doesn’t belong to it.”**

This statement turns a functional problem into an innovation opportunity. Instead of optimizing the conventional spray, Velum explores an alternative relationship between fragrance and skin.

### Traditional model

**Spray → projection → evaporation → reapplication**

### Proposed model

**Stamp → infusion → gradual release → intimate personal expression**

This shift became the foundation for the product, experience and business model developed in the final submission.

## 03. Consumer Signals Used in the Submission

The final presentation includes qualitative social-media observations covering:

- frustration with having to reapply fragrance after a few hours;
- interest in non-alcoholic fragrance propositions that claim extended wear; and
- desire for a scent that feels personally distinctive.

These examples were used as **directional consumer signals**, not as a representative quantitative consumer study.

## 04. Strategic Insight

The strongest insight emerging from the team's framing is the tension between **fragrance as projection** and **fragrance as identity**.

That led to a sharper innovation question:

> **How might L'Oréal transform fragrance from something worn *on* the skin into an experience that feels more integrated *with* the wearer?**

Velum is the team's answer to that question.

## 05. Research Limitation

The final deck does not provide a full methodology, sample size, demographic profile or statistical validation for the cited consumer figures and social-media observations. Therefore, this portfolio page preserves them as **submission evidence and directional signals**, rather than presenting them as independently validated market research.

## 06. What This Demonstrates

This stage of the project demonstrates:

**Signal identification → consumer tension → strategic reframing → innovation opportunity**

The important contribution is not the individual statistic; it is how the team converted multiple unmet needs into one coherent strategic territory for innovation.
