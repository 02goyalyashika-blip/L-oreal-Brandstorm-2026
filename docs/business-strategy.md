# Business Strategy

## 01. Business Model

Velum is positioned as a **refill-driven luxury fragrance platform** rather than a single product launch.

### Revenue architecture

**Hero hardware + fragrance/refill cartridges + DTC + selective retail + experiential activation**

The proposed model is intended to combine premium upfront economics with recurring refill behavior.

## 02. Target Consumer

The final submission identifies three priority segments:

- **Gen Z**
- **Millennials**
- **“Exception luxury” consumers**

The proposition is designed for consumers interested in personalization, premium experiences, beauty technology and more sustainable product formats.

## 03. Go-To-Market

The proposal recommends a **limited luxury pilot launch**, followed by a DTC-led scaling model with selective retail distribution.

Key elements include:

### Experiential retail

The concept proposes experiential activation to make the new ritual understandable and desirable in a premium environment.

### AI-enabled personalization

Personalization is positioned as a service layer around the fragrance experience, extending the **“Beauty for Each”** proposition.

### Fragrance-skincare hybrid positioning

Velum sits at the intersection of fragrance, skin science and beauty technology rather than competing solely as a conventional perfume.

## 04. Commercial Assumptions

The final deck presents the following figures:

| Metric | Competition submission |
| --- | ---: |
| Target market | 125–130 million |
| Indicative unit cost | ~$120 |
| Indicative ASP | $275–$325 |
| Indicative gross margin | 60–80% |
| Break-even timing | Year 1–2 |
| Target long-term users | 650,000 (0.5% capture) |
| Annual spend assumption | $500 |
| Indicative annual revenue | ~$325M |
| Indicative ROI | ~2–3x within 3 years |

These numbers are **concept-stage assumptions contained in the final competition deck**. They should be presented in a portfolio as assumptions, not as independently audited forecasts.

## 05. Unit Economics Logic

The strategic logic is built around a premium hero product that creates an installed hardware base, followed by recurring refill purchases.

This is intended to create:

**Initial purchase → adoption → repeat refill → higher lifetime value**

The final presentation cites a **$400+ customer lifetime value target** as part of the KPI framework.

## 06. Growth KPIs

The proposed KPI set includes:

- **Customer Lifetime Value:** $400+
- **Net Promoter Score:** 50+
- **Growth Rate:** 20–30% monthly
- **Inventory Turnover:** 4–6 times/year

These should be interpreted as **target KPIs for the proposed model**, not reported performance results.

## 07. Sustainability as a Business Lever

Sustainability is integrated into the economics rather than treated only as communication.

The concept proposes:

- refillable cartridges;
- long-life reusable hardware;
- modular kiosks;
- circular design; and
- a lower-waste system.

This structure is intended to increase the useful life of the physical device while creating repeat demand through consumable refills.

## 08. Strategic Fit

The business model supports the wider proposition in three ways:

**Differentiation** — a new fragrance ritual rather than another conventional spray.  
**Recurring economics** — refills create a pathway beyond one-off hardware sales.  
**Brand equity** — experiential, personalized delivery can reinforce a premium L'Oréal Luxe positioning.

## 09. Scale Path

The team positioned the concept around a limited luxury pilot before broader expansion. The long-term ambition described in the submission is to scale toward a large premium fragrance audience while preserving personalization and circularity.

A practical next-stage strategy would therefore be:

**Pilot → validate consumer behavior → validate technology and economics → expand channels → scale ecosystem**

This sequence keeps the highest-risk assumptions testable before full-scale investment.
